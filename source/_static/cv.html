Tennessee Technological University

Dean (2019-present), Professor (2019-present)

Leads a college of approximately 3,300 students and 90 faculty, with an annual non-research operating budget of roughly $26M, $2.5M in research center funding, and approximately $14M/year in additional external research expenditures—the latter having quadrupled over the past 5 years.

  • Created 3 new scholarships:

    • Aspiring Engineering, Computing, & Technology Scholarship for students who transform into superstars after enrolling.
    • Engineering, Computing, & Technology Assistance Grant providing emergency support to students who face dramatic life challenges impacting their ability to attend.
    • College of Engineering Nonresidential Merit-Based Scholarship supporting out-of-state students who are high-achievers yet not eligible for some merit-scholarships that require Tennessee residency.
  • Supported the raising (with a wide-variety of level of contribution from one donor to another) of over $8M towards the Ashraf Islam Engineering Building (including the largest ever cash donation to the university) by creating a strong vision for the building and building strong relationships with donors. After the building was awarded, rebuilt its vision from a collection of unit-owned spaces into a college-wide hub—with labs shared across departments and governed by cross-disciplinary faculty committees—designed to drive student belonging, recruitment, and a sense of home on campus; carried lead design responsibility for the building through that transformation, working with a team of three faculty who developed the building’s programming from that vision.
  • Created the overall vision for and led the funding proposal for the Advanced Construction and Manufacturing Engineering Building (ACME), with three program areas developed by other individuals, delegating lead design responsibility to trusted colleagues while retaining overall project oversight.
  • Invigorated curricular innovation resulting in multiple new minors and concentrations, with three being across multiple colleges.
  • Oversaw the accreditation renewal efforts for seven undergraduate programs in 2020/2021.
  • Oversaw the first accreditation of the joint General Engineering BS program with East Tennessee State University (ETSU) in 2022.
  • Oversaw creation of the Nuclear Engineering BS program.
  • Managed a 3% budget cut in 2020 without resorting to terminations while actually growing faculty in multiple departments.

  • Oversaw the hiring of 113 individuals into faculty or staff positions in the college including:

    • 56 faculty and 57 staff
    • Marketing and Communications director
    • Associate Dean for Academic Affairs
    • Associate Dean for Research
    • Chair of Chemical Engineering
    • Chair of Manufacturing Engineering Technology
    • Chair of Electrical and Computer Engineering
    • Supported hire of Director and Associate Director of General Engineering program
    • Director of MS in Engineering Management
    • Supported the recruitment of multiple highly successful senior faculty
  • Actively engaged multiple employers through advisory board and other direct relationships
  • Expanded the college’s K-12 and community outreach portfolio, adding a VEX Robotics high school competition, an annual Manufacturing Day, and hosting the state Quiz Bowl championship.
  • Centralized college laboratory technicians—previously siloed one-per-department with uneven, mismatched skills—into a shared pool that moves between labs based on skill and central coordination while each retains primary responsibility for one lab, improving time-to-completion, work quality, lab appearance, and lab safety.
  • Introduced normed quantitative performance metrics for use in assessing college, department, and individual performance for use in synergy with qualitative assessments.
  • Supported numerous efforts directly tied to the university’s “Rural Reimagined” grand challenge and numerous efforts in support of the university’s vision and mission.
  • Through the Budget Advisory Committee and other efforts, helped create strategies and practices that will ensure efforts align with university strategic goals and needs.
  • Oversaw the first substantial increases in external expenditures in over eight years through successfully encouraging faculty to reengage with funded research. Expenditures on competitive grants and contracts rose 290% over 6 years (from $3.5M to $13.8M).
  • Redesigned academic advising from an ad hoc mix of faculty and staff advising into a centralized model of dedicated staff advisors paired with faculty mentoring, supporting growth in incoming freshman enrollment by 30% over 6 years, an increase in retention from approximately 70% to over 80%, a marked reduction in erroneous advising, and higher faculty productivity in teaching and research.
  • Restructured college diversity efforts away from a single, siloed DEI officer position toward shared, distributed ownership—giving minority student organizations (SWE, SHPE, NSBE, and oSTEM) direct access to the dean and embedding diversity responsibility across the advising staff—growing the share of students identifying as women from 14.5% to 16%, as Black from 3.5% to 4.1%, and as students of color overall from 11.2% to 15.4%, each outpacing regional trends.
  • Supported the college becoming a National Academy of Engineers Grand Challenge College.
  • Created a co-director position within the college’s Student Success Center focused on holistic student support, and established a donor-funded student emergency fund, with a companion college food pantry established on the initiative of the college’s Student Success Center team and development officer—together providing a discreet, single point of contact for students in crisis and preventing numerous cases of housing loss, food insecurity, or withdrawal from the university.

  • Enacted the first data dissemination college-wide including:
